Abstract

Although the advancement of vision-language models (VLMs) has endowed robots with enhanced environmental understanding and task reasoning, a comprehensive evaluation methodology is important to advance the integration of VLMs in robotic navigation and manipulation. However, current benchmarks lack a comprehensive method to evaluate diverse robotic tasks, and evaluation metrics remain relatively constrained, making it difficult to assess the embodied capabilities of VLMs in a thorough and fine-grained manner.

To address this issue, we propose RMMBench, an evaluation benchmark that requires robots to understand language instructions and perform long-horizon tasks in continuous spaces. RMMBench seamlessly integrates high- and low-level embodied tasks into a unified framework, constructing a “navigation–manipulation” task suite comprising 70 canonical task scenarios that range from localized manipulation to long-horizon composite navigation. The results reveal that leading VLMs still face major challenges in spatial localization when coping with mobile manipulation tasks, and also highlight the necessity of enhancing the spatial perception capability of robots during long-period interactions.

The RMMBench Benchmark

RMMBench requires VLM-based models to execute continuous-space, long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks based on language instructions and raw visual feedback. Evaluating this capability requires tight integration of sequential planning, spatial grounding, and closed-loop visual verification.

Fine-Grained Evaluation Metrics

To provide a rigorous, non-binary evaluation of VLM performance in long-horizon composite tasks, RMMBench provides a quantitative evaluation suite across three core dimensions: task execution progress, visual grounding precision, and error-correction behavior, covering 6 categories of process-oriented metrics.

Execution Efficiency (EE)

Penalizes redundant actions against the expert reference sequence length; computed only over successful trajectories.

Manipulation Completion Rate (CRm)

Measures the proportion of satisfied atomic sub-conditions during manipulation.

Navigation Completion Rate (CRn)

Monitors workspace radius and orientation alignment for navigation stages.

Navigation Progress (NP)

Tracks spatial transit efficiency against the initial expert distance.

Expert Action Recall Rate (RRact)

Formulated through the longest common subsequence (LCS) relative to the expert reference sequence: \( RR_{\text{act}} = \frac{\text{Len}(\text{LCS}(Seq_{\text{vlm}}, Seq_{\text{expert}}))}{\text{Len}(Seq_{\text{expert}})} \). A single-step action counts as recalled if both the skill primitive and the target entity category match the expert reference; for flexibly-ordered tasks, an action matches any valid permutation satisfying the underlying dependency constraints. Expert reference sequences are manually constructed, individually verified, and minimized.

Visual Grounding Score (ASbbox)

Pools all 2D bounding boxes predicted across various execution stages, evaluating alignment resolution via IoU against the ground-truth geometric mask: \( AS_{\text{bbox}} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \text{IoU}(\text{BBox}_k, \text{Mask}_k) \). Malformed or unparsable bounding-box outputs are assigned an IoU of 0.

Reflection Score (RS)

Aggregates all repeated-action loops within an episode. A loop is a sequence of redundant, consecutive manipulation primitives executed on an identical target prior to correcting its prerequisite states (e.g., base positioning). For an episode with \(M \ge 1\) loops of lengths \(n_1, \dots, n_M\): \( RS = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \gamma\, e^{-\lambda n_i} \), with sensitivity \(\lambda > 0\). The compliance coefficient \(\gamma = 1.0\) if the task succeeds and \(\alpha \in (0,1)\) otherwise, ensuring failed trajectories remain bounded below the performance profile of successful ones while preserving flexibility to reward far-horizon exploratory behaviors that circumvent chronic deadlocks. RS is computed only over episodes containing at least one repeated-action loop.

Experiments and Results

Setup

We evaluate five popular VLMs: Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3-Pro-preview, Gemini-3-Flash-preview, Qwen-3.5-Plus, and Doubao-SEED-2.0-Pro-260215. Tests are conducted within a unified MuJoCo simulation framework encompassing 70 canonical task scenarios via domain randomization (60 pure manipulation tasks + 10 composite navigation–manipulation tasks). Each task instance is evaluated over 3 independent episodes to guarantee statistical reliability. Task configurations are pre-generated as fixed JSON files, and all models load identical configurations to ensure fairness and reproducibility.

Implementation details: visual history is capped at ≤8 steps for manipulation trials (≤5 for navigation with BEV); RS hyperparameters are \(\lambda = 0.2\), \(\alpha = 0.5\); episode limits are 15 steps for manipulation and 30 for composite navigation (timeout counts as failure). Serially evaluating all episodes takes approximately 3–4 days; VLMs are invoked through API calls and naturally support parallelization.

The models exhibit comparable performance between standard manipulation and common-sense & semantic reasoning tasks, maintaining a high \(RR_{\text{act}}\) — evidence that they possess rich affordance knowledge for mapping natural language requirements into deterministic action plans. However, for long-horizon manipulation tasks requiring sequential action coordination, the overall success rate decreases. Micro-level analysis reveals this degradation is primarily driven by localized execution failures: when the bounding box drops and triggers an interaction failure, the model repeats the exact same failed action primitive on the target entity instead of using inspection primitives such as wrist camera observations to regain a clear field of view and replan — exposing a critical limitation in dynamic closed-loop error correction and feedback-driven strategy adaptation during extended physical interactions.

Active vision and egocentric navigation. Stripping away global top-down priors (no BEV) leads to a severe degradation in both navigation success rates and pathfinding efficiency across all models. Under purely egocentric constraints, models rely heavily on rotation primitives to keep the target object within the local field of view rather than proactively exploring via forward advancement, drastically increasing action redundancy. Due to the lack of global spatial references, a VLM tends to prematurely issue localized manipulation commands before the mobile base reaches the workstation interaction radius — a vulnerability further exacerbated by subtle obstacles observed in earlier frames but not leveraged for avoidance, highlighting the fragility of egocentric spatial reasoning and temporal visual memory.

Localization accuracy varies in a clearly model-dependent manner as the context lengthens: models such as Gemini-3-Flash remain stable under normal conditions or fluctuate only within a trendless range; only SEED-2.0 exhibits clear monotonic degradation as the context grows, further exacerbated and emerging earlier under the long-distance + occlusion condition. This indicates that context length itself is not the direct cause of localization degradation — the degradation is more likely jointly driven by scene complexity (long-distance and occlusion) and the disparity in visual robustness across models. Models sensitive to complex visual conditions accumulate coordinate hallucinations and grasping failures as the sequence progresses, whereas models with stronger visual robustness maintain stable low-level spatial resolution over long contexts.
